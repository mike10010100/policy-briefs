Series:Practical AI Governance Framework
Prepared by:Michael Paulauski
Date:November 15, 2026
Subject:Worker tax parity, workplace AI rules, and displacement insurance

Bottom line up front

Pew Research finds that 71% of Americans expect AI to reduce total U.S. jobs over the next two decades, with the share of adults under 30 expecting job cuts jumping from 61% in 2024 to 73% in 2026. Yet current U.S. tax law tilts employers toward replacing workers even when automation barely breaks even, while outdated WARN thresholds and expired Trade Adjustment Assistance leave displaced staff without transition income.

Instead of unworkable 'robot taxes' or low-return classroom vouchers, federal policy should narrow the capital-vs-labor tax wedge, set hard workplace dignity and bargaining rights, and build standing wage-loss insurance and paid sectoral career paths.

1. Fix the bonus-depreciation wedge without penalizing open-weight software

Research by Daron Acemoglu, Andrea Manera, and Pascual Restrepo in Brookings Papers on Economic Activity calculates that U.S. tax law imposes a 25.5% to 33.5% effective tax rate on human labor (combining income and payroll taxes, and reaching 33.5% with benefit-phaseout taxes) while taxing software and equipment at roughly 5%, a gap locked back in when the 2025 tax law restored permanent 100% first-year bonus depreciation and immediate domestic software write-offs. That wedge subsidizes "so-so automation" and pushes firms to replace staff with mediocre software largely for the tax break.

Congress should taper large corporate and pass-through 100% bonus depreciation (with anti-leasing rules) toward statutory MACRS schedules while preserving Section 174A R&D expensing and pairing matched recovery for local open-weight inference hardware (so on-prem deployments are not taxed worse than cloud APIs expensed under Section 162, alongside reviewing Section 162 offsets on mass-displacement AI contracts), while keeping small-business Section 179 limits, grid hardware, 35% Section 48D chip-fab credits, and restored clean-energy incentives cut back in July 2025.

Paired with permanent corporate base broadeners (offsetting bonus depreciation's front-loaded timing shift), revenues should fund a gradually phased-out employer-and-worker earnings credit indexed to FICA wages and backed by permanent general-revenue law keeping Social Security and Medicare Trust Funds completely whole.

2. Outlaw abusive "bossware," forced arbitration, and secret deployments

On the job, federal labor law should require documented, independent human override authority for hiring, firing, pay, and work-speed safety across covered employers (including vendor, staffing-agency, and 1099 intermediaries), outlaw injury-inducing algorithmic quotas ("bossware"), and bar forced pre-dispute or separation-agreement arbitration waivers over workplace AI claims so non-union workers can actually enforce statutory protections.

Consistent with the AFL-CIO's Workers First Initiative on AI, Congress should guarantee workers advance notice, vendor audit disclosures, and anti-retaliation protections before covered employers introduce workplace surveillance, quota, or mass-displacement AI.

Under the National Labor Relations Act, Congress should make workplace surveillance and automated quotas a mandatory subject of pre-deployment collective bargaining alongside mandatory effects bargaining over severance, scheduling, and transition protections, backed by civil monetary penalties.

3. Pair federal wage insurance and modernized WARN with paid sectoral training

Tax parity only stops marginal automation; where capable AI agents outperform humans at a fraction of payroll cost, mid-career cognitive workers will still face displacement where legacy retraining programs like the expired Trade Adjustment Assistance system and generic WIOA classroom vouchers failed. Scaling up $10,000 Hamilton Project and Brookings models, Congress should enact federal wage insurance for displaced workers with at least one year of job tenure (subject to a prior-earnings ceiling) that replaces half of the earnings drop (up to an inflation-indexed $15,000 to $20,000 a year for two years) at an unaffiliated new employer.

Congress should also update the federal WARN Act so distributed remote and 180-day rolling mass layoffs trigger 90-day notice (preserving narrow Section 2102(b) faltering-company defenses) and capped mandatory severance, while amending FUTA Section 3304(a) so severance never reduces state unemployment benefits.

For skill transitions and younger entrants, federal workforce funds should shift away from generic classroom vouchers toward paid apprenticeships and employer-backed sectoral training in shortage fields like nursing, grid trades, and public-interest tech (high-performing sectoral models shown to raise multi-year earnings in MDRC randomized trials, led cumulatively over ten years by Per Scholas (+16%) and in Year 10 by St. Nicks Alliance (+32%)), paired with broader FUTA training-approval standards and federally funded extended training UI stipends during full-time programs. Finally, Congress should modernize ERISA multi-employer plan rules so unions and sectoral boards can run portable benefit pools for W-2 workers under joint worker-employer trusteeship and ERISA Section 3(40) MEWA solvency reserves without weakening misclassification laws.

Trade-offs to weigh in policy design

04 · Tax Parity & Reemployment vs. Robot Taxes

Tax economists across the spectrum warn that literal "robot taxes" or domestic AI compute taxes are nearly impossible to define in tax law and risk pushing data centers abroad, while MDRC implementation studies caution that high-performing sectoral training programs are hard to scale rapidly without deep employer commitment. Pairing bonus-depreciation reform and corporate base broadeners with an indexed payroll credit and rapid-reemployment wage insurance cushions displaced workers without taxing domestic compute or stranding laid-off staff in low-return classes.

Appendix: Plain-English technical and policy glossary

3 plain-language definitions for statutory, technical, and institutional terms used in Brief 2026-01D.

100% bonus depreciation vs. statutory MACRS ("so-so automation")
Bonus depreciation (IRC Section 168(k)) lets businesses deduct the full cost of new and eligible used equipment and purchased software in Year 1 instead of spreading deductions across statutory MACRS or 36-month Section 167(f) schedules. Section 174A covers domestic R&D (excluding GPU hardware under Section 174A(d)(1)), while cloud API subscriptions are deducted as operating expenses under Section 162. Economists use "so-so automation" for mediocre systems deployed mainly because capital write-offs are taxed far more lightly than human wages.
"Bossware," wage insurance, and sectoral training
"Bossware" is workplace software that monitors keystrokes, cameras, or movement speed to enforce rigid quotas or automated discipline. Wage insurance is a temporary federal paycheck supplement (such as covering half of a pay drop up to an indexed $15,000-$20,000 annual cap for two years) when a displaced worker starts a new job at lower pay. Sectoral training pairs public workforce funds directly with employers and unions to train and place workers into specific shortage occupations.
WARN Act, FUTA, and ERISA multi-employer plans
The federal WARN Act (29 U.S.C. Section 2101) currently requires employers with 100 or more full-time workers to give 60 days' notice before plant closings affecting 50 or more full-time workers, or single-site mass layoffs affecting at least 500 full-time workers (or 50 to 499 workers making up at least one-third of active full-time site staff) within a 30-day period. FUTA (the Federal Unemployment Tax Act, 26 U.S.C. Section 3304) sets baseline federal UI rules, including training-approval and benefit-offset conditions. Under ERISA and Taft-Hartley Section 302(c)(5), portable multiemployer health and pension pools remain largely tied to union bargaining agreements while non-union MEWA pools face ERISA Section 3(40) solvency risks without statutory reserves.
