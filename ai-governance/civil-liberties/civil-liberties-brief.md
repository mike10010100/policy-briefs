**ISSUE BRIEF**
Series: Practical AI Governance Framework (Brief 2026-01C)
Prepared by: Michael Paulauski
Date: November 15, 2026
Subject: Statutory civil-liberties, courtroom, and patient-care guardrails

### Bottom line up front

As the 2026 Pentagon vendor dispute analyzed by Just Security and the Electronic Frontier Foundation showed, constitutional and human-rights boundaries cannot rest on private contract standoffs between one AI lab and defense officials. Meanwhile, black-box algorithms are increasingly shaping policing, public benefits, bail, and health-insurance coverage while low-income Americans face a severe shortage of human legal counsel.

Congress should enact bright-line statutory rules governing defense autonomy and domestic surveillance, separate vetted citizen legal-aid access from coercive state scoring, and stop health insurers and Medicare contractors from using algorithms to ration care.

### 1. Codify human command over lethal force and close surveillance loopholes

Congress should enact binding defense appropriations and fielding prohibitions requiring meaningful human verification and command authorization over anti-personnel target selection and offensive lethal force (barring automated kill-list rubber-stamping while preserving point-defense counter-munitions against incoming projectiles), alongside a binding statutory ban on autonomous AI in nuclear command, control, and launch decisions.

For domestic policing and intelligence, Congress should require judicial warrants (subject only to narrow, audited imminent-life-safety exigencies and backed by statutory suppression remedies) for government AI facial-recognition and networked camera dragnets, and enact the Fourth Amendment Is Not For Sale Act to close the commercial data-broker loophole documented by the ACLU and GAO, where intelligence and police agencies buy bulk phone-location and web-browsing records without a warrant.

### 2. Fund indigent defense and audited legal aid while barring black-box scoring

Inside the justice system, policy should separate citizen access from institutional coercion. The Legal Services Corporation's Justice Gap Report finds that 92% of low-income Americans' substantial civil legal problems receive inadequate or no legal help. Alongside expanded funding to hire human legal-aid attorneys and public defenders, Congress should expand Legal Services Corporation and State Justice Institute technology grants for vetted civil tools, like those piloted at Stanford's Legal Design Lab, that help low-income tenants and veterans handle evictions, consumer debt, and benefits claims.

Because federal law bars LSC from criminal cases, Congress should pair civil legal-aid funding with ring-fenced Department of Justice Byrne JAG public-defender grants and federal Judiciary Defender Services funding so overworked defense lawyers can use zero-retention, privilege-protected, audited AI to review criminal discovery files.

At the same time, federal statute should bar unreviewed automated or black-box algorithms from federal sentencing and federally funded benefits denials (including Medicaid, SNAP, and veterans' benefits), while conditioning state Byrne JAG funds on barring algorithmic risk-assessment scores in pre-trial detention, bail, and sentencing. Congress and the Judicial Conference should also finish reworking the Rule 707 draft withdrawn in May 2026 and companion Rule 901(c) deepfake-authentication rules to set strict Daubert-grade reliability standards for criminal and civil trials without pricing out or blocking authentic bystander recordings.

### 3. Ban algorithmic health-care denials and vendor denial bounties

Following federal lawsuits over Medicare Advantage prior-authorization algorithms and the unanimous June 2026 House Appropriations Committee vote to defund Original Medicare's six-state WISeR pilot, Congress should halt WISeR and direct HHS and the Department of Labor to set an enforceable patient-review standard across public and private coverage.

Federal statute and agency rules should prohibit Medicare Advantage and Medicaid managed-care plans, federal Medicare contractors, Federal Employees Health Benefits plans, ERISA group health plans, and commercial insurers under the Public Health Service Act from denying medical necessity or cutting off post-acute care by algorithm without documented, individualized review of the patient record by a licensed physician or qualified health-care specialist acting within clinical scope of practice (while permitting automated fast-track approvals).

Rules should equally ban denial-based bounties or savings quotas for both outside contractors and in-house insurer reviewers, and pair a federal statutory remedy with an explicit savings clause so ERISA (including self-funded plans), Medicare Advantage, and FEHBA preemption never shield unlawful algorithmic care denials from state bad-faith accountability.

### Trade-offs to weigh in policy design

**03 · Courtroom Access vs. Hallucination Risk**
State court administrators and the National Center for State Courts warn that low-income litigants rarely have a second lawyer to catch AI hallucinations, which empirical tests by Stanford researchers still find in commercial legal tools. Publicly funded legal-aid software must carry mandatory independent accuracy audits, court-approved, attorney-vetted template bounds for pro se self-help tools, and human attorney sign-off on open-ended generative court briefs.

### Appendix: Plain-English technical and policy glossary

#### Surveillance, courts, and legal aid

**Byrne JAG and LSC legal-aid funding**
Byrne JAG is the primary Department of Justice grant program supporting state and local criminal-justice systems (including indigent public defense), whereas the Legal Services Corporation (LSC) funds civil legal aid for low-income households and is barred by federal statute from criminal cases.

**Commercial data-broker loophole**
The practice where intelligence agencies and police departments buy bulk phone-location, web-browsing, and personal records from commercial data brokers on the open market to track Americans without getting a Fourth Amendment court warrant.

**Daubert reliability, withdrawn Proposed Rule 707, and Rule 901(c)**
Daubert is the federal courtroom standard requiring expert technical evidence to prove reliable methods before reaching a jury. After withdrawing Proposed Rule 707 in May 2026 for refinement, the Judicial Conference Advisory Committee on Evidence Rules is developing updated machine-generated evidence reliability standards alongside Rule 901(c) procedures for challenging suspected deepfakes.

**AI hallucinations in legal software**
Hallucinations are plausible-sounding false facts, invented citations, or misstated holdings produced because generative AI predicts token patterns, a failure mode that persists even when legal tools retrieve verified case records.

#### Health insurance and Medicare

**Medicare Advantage prior authorization and WISeR**
Prior authorization requires insurer sign-off before a clinician's prescribed treatment or ongoing post-acute care is covered. WISeR (Wasteful and Inappropriate Service Reduction) is CMS's 2026 six-state pilot (AZ, NJ, OH, OK, TX, WA) introducing private AI prior-authorization contractors paid a share of averted spending into traditional Original Medicare.
