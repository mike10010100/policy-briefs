Bottom line up front

Across the PJM grid spanning 13 states and Washington, D.C., PJM's Independent Market Monitor calculates that surging data-center demand forecasts added $9.33 billion to the 2025/2026 wholesale capacity auction, while U.S. Department of Energy resource-adequacy filings warn that rapid large-load growth is colliding with plant retirements and driving up household electric bills. Meanwhile, single dry-basin campuses pull millions of gallons of municipal water daily or run trailer-mounted gas turbines outside major-source air permits.

Federal and state policy should enforce strict cost causation: require large facilities to fund grid upgrades up front, unlock clean interconnection queues through modest peak flexibility and surplus-capacity reuse, condition federal water and energy aid on closed-loop, hybrid adiabatic, or reclaimed cooling, and close methane-turbine air permitting loopholes.

Section 01 · Transmission & Capacity Rates

1. Require 100% cost-causation tariffs with minimum-term collateral

Through FERC wholesale transmission and capacity rules, Department of Energy grid-grant conditions, and state public utility commission rate standards, policymakers should require 100% cost-causation tariffs paired with minimum-term financial collateral for hyperscale loads (such as facilities or campuses over 20 megawatts).

Mandatory collateral ensures residential customers and small businesses never subsidize Big Tech substation buildouts or inherit stranded grid debt if a speculative project cancels, consolidates workloads elsewhere, or misses load forecasts.

Section 02 · Clean Power, Curtailment & Reconductoring

2. Pair clean-power interconnection priority with peak load curtailment

Congress and FERC should reserve fast-track interconnection queues for large facilities that bring new, deliverable clean generation on a rising hourly-matched schedule (using near-term solar and battery storage while scaling enhanced geothermal and nuclear) and agree to trim non-urgent runs, shift regional workloads, or draw clean on-site storage without running diesel or fossil-methane backup by 0.25% to 1% of annual electricity use (mostly partial drawdowns across roughly 85 to 366 peak grid-stress hours a year, equal to 22 to 88 full-load hours).

Companion 2025 and 2026 studies from Duke University's Nicholas Institute find that curtailing grid withdrawals during those peak hours unlocks 76 to 126 gigawatts of headroom across U.S. grids (which federal rules should pair with a strict ban on fossil backup shifting), while broader data-center load flexibility scaling up to 50% of flexible hours cuts U.S. power-system capital, operating, and fuel costs by $40 billion to $150 billion over ten years (verified against peak curtailment dispatch telemetry).

Congress should also authorize FERC to speed up advanced transmission reconductoring and dynamic line ratings on existing corridors, and prioritize non-emitting power and storage interconnections that reuse spare hookup capacity at operating plants under Order 845 or retiring coal sites under a nationwide generator-replacement rule.

Section 03 · Water Conservation & Air Permitting

3. Condition federal water funds and close methane-turbine air loopholes

On water, Water Foundation and Brookings analyses show that a single hyperscale facility can draw 1 million to 5 million gallons of treated water a day in dry basins. Because federal agencies cannot set municipal water rates directly, Congress should condition future EPA State Revolving Fund and Bureau of Reclamation water-project grants, while the executive branch conditions discretionary DOE loans and facilities serving federal cloud contracts, on ending subsidized potable water discounts for large data centers.

Those funding conditions should require zero-evaporation closed-loop, hybrid adiabatic, or reclaimed municipal wastewater cooling in water-stressed basins, paired with automatic drought cutbacks on any remaining potable draw.

On air pollution, Congress and the EPA should close loopholes for methane turbines powering fixed data centers by repealing the 2026 Subpart KKKKa 24-month temporary-turbine (25 ppm NOx) and sub-45% capacity-factor SCR subcategories for dedicated data-center generation, blocking EPA's proposed Nonroad Combustion Turbines Rule (RIN 2060-AX08) and barring nonroad-engine claims, counting co-located turbines together under major-source limits, codifying Michigan v. DOE limits against multi-year Federal Power Act Section 202(c) coal and gas retirement overrides, and statutorily barring Clean Air Act Section 110(f) and Section 112(i)(4) waivers for commercial data centers. That ends the unpermitted gas-turbine and emergency fossil practices documented in NAACP, Southern Environmental Law Center, and Earthjustice filings in South Memphis, Southaven, Mississippi, and federal grid dockets.

Implementation Design

Trade-offs to weigh in policy design

01 · Permitting Scope & Community Safeguards

Fast-tracking clean energy infrastructure can fracture center-left coalitions if permitting bills sweep too broadly, as Time documented during the 2025 and 2026 permitting battles. Statutory fast-tracks must stay tightly scoped to interregional transmission timelines, reconductoring, dynamic line ratings, geothermal exploration, and battery or zero-carbon generation on low-conflict or remediated sites, while preserving Tribal consultation and cumulative fenceline safeguards everywhere and never granting fast-tracks or weaker NEPA and Clean Air Act enforcement to fossil projects, as demanded by the Sierra Club and frontline community groups.

Power grid and interconnection

PJM grid and wholesale capacity auctions
PJM Interconnection is America's largest regional grid operator, managing wholesale electricity across 13 Mid-Atlantic and Midwest states plus Washington, D.C. Its capacity auctions pay power plants in advance to guarantee enough supply during future peak demand; surging data-center power forecasts have driven those auction prices sharply higher on household bills.
FERC, state utility commissions, and 100% cost-causation tariffs
FERC (the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission) regulates interstate electricity transmission and wholesale power markets, while state public utility commissions set retail electricity rates. A 100% cost-causation tariff is a wholesale and retail rate rule requiring a giant new electricity user, such as a hyperscale data center, to pay the full cost of the grid upgrades and power capacity it triggers so residential bills do not rise.
Interconnection queues, Order 845, and generator replacement
The interconnection queue is the multi-year engineering waitlist new power projects and large loads face before plugging into the grid. FERC Order 845 ("surplus interconnection") lets new generation or batteries share unused hookup capacity at an operating power plant, while generator-replacement rules let new projects reuse the existing grid hookup when an older plant retires; both are currently fuel-neutral rules that Congress and FERC can pair with clean-power priority and a nationwide replacement standard at operating or retiring fossil sites.
Peak load curtailment
A binding grid agreement where a data center trims non-urgent computing jobs, shifts regional workloads, or draws clean on-site battery storage (without running diesel or fossil-methane backup generators) during the grid's highest-stress summer and winter hours so utilities do not have to build billions of dollars in extra fossil-fuel peaker plants.
Advanced reconductoring and dynamic line ratings
Advanced reconductoring replaces old steel-core wires on existing transmission towers with modern composite-core cables that carry roughly twice as much electricity without multi-year land fights for new towers. Dynamic line ratings use real-time wind and temperature sensors so grid operators can safely send more power through existing wires on cool or breezy days.
Enhanced geothermal
Clean baseload energy that uses modern directional rock-drilling techniques to tap natural heat miles underground and generate steady 24/7 carbon-free electricity with a small land footprint.

Water cooling and air permitting

Closed-loop, hybrid adiabatic, and reclaimed-water cooling
Closed-loop chillers recirculate fluid in sealed heat exchangers with zero evaporative water loss (at the cost of slightly higher hot-afternoon electricity draw), while hybrid adiabatic systems run dry most of the year and use evaporative misting only on extreme heat hours. Reclaimed (recycled) water is treated municipal wastewater piped to industrial campuses for non-potable cooling instead of drawing local drinking water.
Methane-turbine permitting loopholes (Subpart KKKKa and nonroad claims)
Fixed data centers have run modular or trailer-mounted methane gas turbines outside major-source pollution controls by claiming Clean Air Act nonroad-engine status meant for movable machinery (which EPA's RIN 2060-AX08 proposal would widen) or using EPA's January 2026 Subpart KKKKa temporary (up to 24 months) and low-capacity-factor stationary turbine subcategories.
