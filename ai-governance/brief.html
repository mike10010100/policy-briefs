Policy Brief Series · Executive Overview
Series:Series 2026-01 · Practical AI governance framework
Prepared by:Michael Paulauski
Date:November 15, 2026
Subject:Executive overview and talking points across six federal AI pillars

Bottom line up front

Polling from the Pew Research Center and community-backlash research from the Brookings Institution show why voters across regions are demanding stronger AI rules: rising electric bills, dry-basin water strain, unpermitted gas turbines near neighborhoods, workplace surveillance, algorithmic rent and insurance denials, and anxiety over career displacement (with 71% expecting fewer total U.S. jobs, led by 73% among adults under 30). Families and clinicians are also sounding alarms over manipulative youth AI companions and lab safety corner-cutting. At the same time, Stanford AI Index, CSIS, and Lowy Institute tracking confirms that unilateral U.S. software freezes fail across borders: DeepSeek and Alibaba narrowed frontier benchmark gaps to roughly 2.7% and gained wide global adoption through open weights.

The governing principle is to separate open research speed from accountable deployment speed: let American researchers, universities, and startups build without Big Tech bottlenecks while enforcing binding public-interest rules wherever commercial AI touches the power grid, civil rights, courts, health care, paychecks, creative work, catastrophic bio-cyber risks, and minors.

1. Make hyperscale data centers fund clean power and water up front

Families never asked to bankroll Big Tech server farms through higher utility bills, depleted aquifers, or unpermitted neighborhood gas plants. Every facility or campus over 20 megawatts should pay its full infrastructure and pollution tab up front.

  • Enforce 100% cost-causation grid tariffs with minimum-term collateral across FERC, DOE grid grants, and state utility commissions so stranded data-center debt never lands on household bills.
  • Reserve fast-track interconnection queues for new, deliverable clean power on an hourly-matched schedule (solar and batteries now, scaling geothermal and nuclear) paired with 0.25% to 1% peak load curtailment across 85 to 366 peak stress hours (unlocking 76 to 126 gigawatts of U.S. grid-withdrawal headroom, paired with a strict ban on fossil backup shifting) alongside reconductoring and dynamic line ratings and clean-priority Order 845 / generator replacement at operating or retiring coal sites.
  • Condition federal water grants (Congress) and DOE loans/cloud contracts in water-stressed basins on ending potable water discounts, requiring drought cutbacks, and mandating closed-loop, hybrid adiabatic, or reclaimed municipal wastewater cooling, and close Subpart KKKKa, RIN 2060-AX08 nonroad, Clean Air Act Sections 110(f)/112(i)(4), and multi-year FPA Section 202(c) methane-turbine loopholes for commercial data centers.

Full issue brief (Brief 2026-01A): Clean power, water, and air rules for hyperscale data centers (power-water/power-water-brief.pdf)

2. Break Big Tech compute bottlenecks and ban algorithmic price-fixing

Four or five hyperscale cloud conglomerates should not control who gets to train AI or use shared pricing engines to squeeze renters and shoppers.

  • Expand NSF and DOE's Sept. 2026 NAIRR Operations Center via the bipartisan CREATE AI Act so universities, teaching hospitals, civic groups, and startups run open-science R&D and public-interest compute plus vendor-neutral cloud vouchers instead of donated Big Tech credits.
  • Keep open-weight AI models below Pillar 6's catastrophic bio-cyber tripwires legal to inspect and deploy on local or domestic servers instead of forcing institutions onto permanent closed corporate APIs.
  • Block exclusive cloud-compute tying and accelerator allocation bottlenecks, enact modernized HSR premerger review for disguised acqui-hires, and outlaw shared algorithms training on current or historical nonpublic competitor data (closing RealPage's 12-month loophole) while sparing proprietary single-merchant tools using a firm's own operational plus public data.

Full issue brief (Brief 2026-01B): Public compute, open-weight models, and algorithmic antitrust (compute-antitrust/compute-antitrust-brief.pdf)

3. Set statutory civil-liberties, courtroom, and patient-care guardrails while expanding indigent defense and vetted legal-aid tools

Constitutional limits cannot depend on private vendor contracts, and technology should expand human access to justice without automating surveillance, sentencing, or insurance denials.

  • Enact binding defense appropriations requiring human command verification over anti-personnel lethal force (preserving point-defense counter-munitions), ban autonomous nuclear launch, require judicial warrants (with narrow life-safety exigencies) for biometric dragnets, and pass the Fourth Amendment Is Not For Sale Act.
  • Boost human legal-aid and public-defender hiring alongside audited LSC/SJI civil tools and privilege-protected Byrne JAG / Defender Services discovery AI, while barring algorithmic bail, sentencing, or benefits denials and reworking the withdrawn Rule 707 draft and Rule 901(c) deepfake-evidence rules.
  • Halt Original Medicare's WISeR pilot and ban automated coverage denials across Medicare, Medicaid, FEHB, ERISA, and commercial plans without specialty physician or qualified clinician review (permitting automated fast-track approvals), ban denial bounties, and override ERISA, Medicare Advantage, and FEHBA preemption to preserve state bad-faith remedies.

Full issue brief (Brief 2026-01C): Statutory civil-liberties, courtroom, and patient-care guardrails (civil-liberties/civil-liberties-brief.pdf)

4. Equalize worker taxes, ban abusive "bossware," and insure workers against agentic displacement

Tax law should stop paying corporations a bonus to replace human staff solely for tax arbitrage, and workers need concrete income security and bargaining power when capable AI agents transform white-collar roles.

  • Scale back corporate 100% bonus depreciation toward economic depreciation while keeping Section 174A R&D, small-business Section 179, grid hardware, domestic chip fabs, and restored clean-energy incentives, funding a payroll credit backed by mandatory dollar-for-dollar general-revenue transfers keeping Social Security and Medicare whole.
  • Require an accountable human decision-maker, outlaw injury-inducing algorithmic quotas ("bossware") and forced arbitration, mandate advance notice and anti-retaliation protections, and establish NLRA pre-deployment bargaining over surveillance and displacement.
  • Replace broken classroom-only retraining with 50% federal wage insurance ($15,000-$20,000/yr cap for two years), 90-day remote/rolling WARN Act notice with severance, paid apprenticeships and sectoral training in shortage fields, and union and sectoral ERISA portable benefit pools (without weakening W-2 misclassification laws).

Full issue brief (Brief 2026-01D): Worker tax parity, workplace AI rules, and displacement insurance (workers-taxes/workers-taxes-brief.pdf)

5. Close civil deepfake gaps, stop voice-clone fraud, and protect creator rights

Voters and working artists deserve real recourse against sexualized deepfakes, senior voice-clone scams, and uncompensated piracy without censoring parody or tracking everyday users.

  • Pair the 2025 TAKE IT DOWN Act (Pub. L. 119-12, with bad-faith takedown safeguards) with DEFIANCE Act civil suits against intimate-deepfake perpetrators and NO FAKES Act voice-and-likeness rights with parody/news/host safe harbors and anti-clickwrap protections.
  • Enforce FTC Part 461 and FCC AI-voice rules plus telecom caller-ID authentication against senior scams, require hosted commercial media generators (never open-weight files or text) to embed open privacy-clean provenance retained across platform transcoding, and impose zero penalties on unsigned citizen media.
  • Mandate commercial training-data summaries, penalize pirated shadow libraries and commercial laundering of academic datasets, and back voluntary collective licensing paying individual creators without erasing fair use.

Full issue brief (Brief 2026-01E): Intimate-deepfake remedies, voice-clone fraud, and creator rights (deepfakes-creators/deepfakes-creators-brief.pdf)

6. Govern deployment pace, frontier bio-cyber risks, and youth chatbots without surveillance IDs

Answering real fears about AI safety, children, and unchecked speed means putting strict design duties and physical choke points in law while refusing intrusive government-ID checkpoints.

  • Protect open R&D speed while enforcing sector-specific pre-deployment safety standards in high-stakes domains, paired with NIST/DOE export-controlled cluster compute-verification research (excluding consumer GPUs).
  • Pass the AI Whistleblower Protection Act (S. 1792), authorize non-regulatory NIST CAISI, set a floor-only federal SB 53 / RAISE baseline ($500M / 10^26 FLOPs, 72-hour critical and 24-hour imminent incident notices, preserving stronger state remedies), require capability-gated bio-cyber red-teaming (banning general compute licensing), and enact expanded statutory NSTM-6 encrypted gene-synthesis screening.
  • Outlaw deceptive human/therapist impersonation, minor-directed sexualized or romantic roleplay, self-harm reinforcement, and retention loops on commercial companion bots and companion modes (with private suits), while banning KYC IDs on general AI and requiring blind-signed, zero-attestation cryptographic age tokens where adult checks apply.

Trade-offs to weigh in policy design

01 · Permitting Scope

Fast-tracking clean energy infrastructure can fracture center-left coalitions if bills sweep too broadly. Statutory fast-tracks must stay strictly scoped to transmission timelines, reconductoring, dynamic line ratings, geothermal, and battery/zero-carbon projects on low-conflict or remediated sites, preserving Tribal consultation and cumulative fenceline safeguards everywhere and never granting fast-tracks or weaker NEPA and Clean Air Act enforcement to fossil projects, as demanded by the Sierra Club and frontline community groups.

02 · Tax Parity vs. Robot Taxes

Tax economists across the spectrum warn that literal 'robot taxes' or compute levies are nearly impossible to define and risk driving investment abroad, while MDRC trials caution that sectoral training requires deep employer commitment to scale. Scaling back general corporate bonus depreciation (while protecting Section 174A R&D) funds payroll relief and wage insurance immediately without taxing domestic compute.

03 · Open Weights vs. Frontier Tripwires

Keeping model weights open checks Big Tech lock-in. Pre-release testing should apply only above efficiency-indexed catastrophic bio/cyber tripwires with an explicit safe harbor for open weights below those limits (never shielding CSAM, shadow-library piracy, or intimate deepfakes), paired with physical NSTM-6 DNA screening.

04 · Courtroom Access vs. Hallucinations

Low-income litigants rarely have a second lawyer to catch AI hallucinations that Stanford studies still document in legal tools. Publicly funded legal-aid software requires independent accuracy audits, attorney-vetted pro se templates, and human attorney sign-off on generative briefs.

05 · Likeness Rights & Creator Pay vs. Free Expression

Overbroad likeness or watermark laws can chill satire, reporting, and open tools. Limiting synthetic provenance to hosted commercial media without identity tracking, barring penalties on unsigned open media, protecting parody/news/fair use, and targeting shadow-library piracy protects working creators cleanly.

06 · Youth Companion Safety vs. KYC IDs

Bills like the GUARD Act risk creating centralized ID/biometric dragnets post-Paxton that break on open operating systems and invite NetChoice injunctions where age gates burden non-erotic speech. Regulating manipulative companion product design directly and requiring zero-retention, non-linkable age tokens protects both kids and user privacy.
