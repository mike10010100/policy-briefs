Bottom line up front

Polling from the Pew Research Center and community-backlash research from the Brookings Institution point to concrete household pressures driving public frustration over AI: rising home electric bills, local water strain, workplace surveillance, and job anxiety. Families are also confronting algorithmic rent and health-insurance abuses alongside unpermitted data-center power plants near residential neighborhoods.

Yet trying to bottle up AI software at the U.S. border will fail, and hardware export controls alone cannot preserve an American lead. Benchmark tracking in the Stanford AI Index and strategic assessments from CSIS and the Lowy Institute show that despite U.S. chip restrictions, Chinese labs such as DeepSeek and Alibaba have narrowed frontier benchmark gaps to single digits and gained wide global adoption through open-weight releases. Pew also finds that 71% of Americans expect AI to reduce total U.S. jobs over the next two decades, with the sharpest jump in concern among adults under 30 (climbing from 61% in 2024 to 73% in 2026) amid tighter entry-level white-collar hiring.

Core Federal Goal

The federal policy goal is straightforward: let American researchers and startups build at full speed, require hyperscale tech operators to fund clean infrastructure up front, build public and open alternatives to Big Tech monopolies, set statutory civil-liberties and patient-care limits on automated decisions, and pair pro-worker tax and labor rules with real income and career paths when capable AI agents displace mid-career staff or freeze out younger entrants.

Recommendation 01 · Clean Power, Water & Air

1. Make hyperscale data centers fund clean power and water up front

Across the PJM grid spanning 13 states and Washington, D.C., U.S. Department of Energy filings and PJM's Independent Market Monitor show that surging data center load has collided with power plant retirements, adding more than $9 billion in data-center capacity costs to a single wholesale auction and pushing up household electric bills. Through FERC wholesale transmission and capacity rules, Department of Energy grid-grant conditions, and state utility commission rate standards, policymakers should require 100% cost-causation tariffs for hyperscale loads (such as facilities over 20 megawatts) so families never subsidize Big Tech grid upgrades.

Congress and FERC should reserve fast-track interconnection queues for large facilities that bring deliverable, hourly-matched clean generation (using near-term solar and battery storage while scaling enhanced geothermal and nuclear) and agree to trim non-urgent runs or shift to clean on-site storage (without running diesel backup) by 0.25% to 1% of annual electricity use (mostly partial drawdowns across roughly 85 to 366 peak grid-stress hours a year, equal to 22 to 88 full-load hours). Companion 2025 and 2026 studies from Duke University's Nicholas Institute find that this modest peak curtailment unlocks 76 to 126 gigawatts of headroom across U.S. grids, while broader data center load flexibility cuts U.S. power-system capital and operating costs by $40 billion to $150 billion over ten years. FERC and Congress should also speed up advanced transmission reconductoring and prioritize non-emitting power and storage interconnections that reuse spare hookup capacity at operating plants under Order 845 or retiring coal sites under generator-replacement rules.

On water and air, Water Foundation and Brookings analyses show that a single hyperscale facility can draw 1 million to 5 million gallons of treated water a day in dry basins. Because federal agencies cannot set city water rates directly, Congress should condition future EPA State Revolving Fund and Bureau of Reclamation water-project grants, while the executive branch conditions discretionary DOE loans and facilities serving federal cloud contracts, on ending subsidized potable water discounts for large data centers and requiring closed-loop or reclaimed municipal wastewater cooling in water-stressed basins with automatic drought cutbacks.

Congress and the EPA should also close Clean Air Act loopholes for trailer-mounted or "temporary" methane turbines powering fixed data centers by repealing the 2026 Subpart KKKKa 24-month temporary-turbine exemption, barring nonroad-engine claims, and statutorily barring Clean Air Act Section 112(i)(4) "national security" or emergency waivers for commercial data centers. That ends the unpermitted gas-turbine practices documented in NAACP, Southern Environmental Law Center, and Earthjustice filings in South Memphis and Southaven, Mississippi.

Recommendation 02 · Public Compute & Antitrust

2. Break Big Tech compute bottlenecks and ban algorithmic price-fixing

Leaving frontier AI in the hands of four or five cloud conglomerates concentrates too much economic and political power. Building on NSF's 2026 launch of the NAIRR Operations Center with the Department of Energy, Congress should pass the bipartisan CREATE AI Act and appropriate multi-billion-dollar funding for publicly owned compute at national labs and university supercomputing centers so researchers, public hospitals, and independent startups get real capacity without relying on donated Big Tech cloud credits. Federal policy should also keep open-weight AI models legal so smaller firms and civic institutions can run software on local hardware without paying permanent rent to closed corporate APIs.

On antitrust, the FTC and DOJ should block exclusive cloud-compute tying, scrutinize chip allocation bottlenecks, and police "acqui-hire" deals structured to dodge merger review. Building on DOJ's RealPage consent decrees, Congress should also pass the Preventing Algorithmic Collusion Act to outlaw shared pricing algorithms that pool nonpublic competitor data or coordinate competitor pricing so competing landlords and corporations cannot inflate rents and consumer prices in lockstep.

Recommendation 03 · Civil Liberties, Courts & Health Care

3. Set statutory civil-liberties, courtroom, and patient-care guardrails while funding legal aid AI

As the 2026 Pentagon vendor dispute analyzed by Just Security and the Electronic Frontier Foundation showed, constitutional boundaries cannot rest on private contract standoffs between one AI lab and defense officials. Congress should pass statutory requirements for affirmative human control over lethal force decisions, turn the FY2025 defense bill's non-binding nuclear guardrail into a binding statutory ban on autonomous AI in nuclear launch decisions, and enact the Fourth Amendment Is Not For Sale Act to close the commercial data-broker loophole documented by the ACLU and GAO, where intelligence and police agencies buy bulk phone-location records without a warrant.

Inside the justice system, policy should separate citizen access from institutional coercion. The Legal Services Corporation's Justice Gap Report finds that 92% of low-income Americans' civil legal problems receive inadequate or no legal help. Alongside expanded funding to hire human legal-aid attorneys and public defenders, Congress should expand Legal Services Corporation and State Justice Institute technology grants for vetted civil tools, like those piloted at Stanford's Legal Design Lab, that help low-income tenants and veterans handle evictions, consumer debt, and benefits claims. Because federal law bars LSC from criminal cases, Congress should pair this with ring-fenced Department of Justice Byrne JAG public-defender grants and federal Judiciary Defender Services funding so overworked defense lawyers can use audited AI to review criminal discovery files.

At the same time, federal statute should bar black-box algorithms from federal sentencing and federally funded benefits denials (including Medicaid, SNAP, and veterans' benefits), while conditioning state Byrne JAG funds on barring opaque bail and sentencing scores. Congress and the Judicial Conference should finish reworking the withdrawn 2026 Rule 707 draft and related evidence rules to set strict Daubert-level reliability and deepfake-authentication standards for trial evidence without blocking authentic bystander recordings.

In health care, following lawsuits over Medicare Advantage prior-authorization algorithms and congressional scrutiny of Original Medicare's 2026 six-state WISeR pilot, Congress, HHS, and the Department of Labor should prohibit Medicare Advantage and Medicaid managed-care plans, federal Medicare contractors, Federal Employees Health Benefits plans, ERISA group health plans, and commercial insurers under the Public Health Service Act from denying medical necessity by algorithm without documented, individualized review of the patient record by a licensed physician in a relevant specialty, and ban paying review vendors based on care denials or savings.

Recommendation 04 · Worker Taxes, Bossware & Wage Insurance

4. Equalize worker taxes, ban abusive "bossware," and insure workers against agentic displacement

Research by Daron Acemoglu, Andrea Manera, and Pascual Restrepo in Brookings Papers on Economic Activity calculates that U.S. tax law imposes a 25.5% to 33.5% effective tax rate on human labor (combining income and payroll taxes, and reaching 33.5% with benefit-phaseout taxes) while taxing software and equipment at roughly 5%, a gap locked back in when the 2025 tax law restored permanent 100% first-year bonus depreciation and immediate domestic software write-offs. That wedge subsidizes "so-so automation" and pushes firms to replace staff with mediocre software largely for the tax break.

Congress should scale back corporate 100% bonus depreciation and amortize large-firm enterprise-automation software write-offs toward economic depreciation (preserving genuine scientific R&D and small-startup software expensing under Section 174A, small-business Section 179 hardware limits, clean energy, grid hardware, and domestic chip fabs) and use that corporate revenue to help fund a refundable working-class payroll tax credit with a mandatory dollar-for-dollar general-revenue transfer that keeps the Social Security and Medicare Trust Funds whole.

On the job, federal labor law should require an accountable human decision-maker for hiring, firing, pay, and work-speed safety, while outlawing injury-inducing algorithmic quotas ("bossware"). Consistent with the AFL-CIO's Workers First Initiative on AI, Congress should guarantee all workers advance notice, algorithmic disclosures, and anti-retaliation protections before employers introduce workplace surveillance, quota, or job-displacing AI, and make those deployments a mandatory subject of collective bargaining under the National Labor Relations Act.

Tax parity only stops marginal automation; once capable AI agents outperform humans at a fraction of payroll cost, mid-career cognitive workers will still face displacement where legacy retraining programs like the expired Trade Adjustment Assistance system and generic WIOA classroom vouchers failed. Building on Hamilton Project and Brookings wage-insurance models, Congress should enact federal wage insurance for displaced workers with at least one year of job tenure that temporarily replaces half of the earnings drop (up to an inflation-indexed annual cap such as $15,000 to $20,000 for two years) when they take a lower-paying job with a new employer. Congress should also update the federal WARN Act so distributed remote and rolling white-collar AI layoffs trigger 90-day notice and mandatory severance transition funding without reducing state unemployment benefits.

For skill transitions and younger entrants, federal workforce funds should shift away from generic classroom vouchers toward paid apprenticeships and employer-backed sectoral training in shortage fields like nursing, grid trades, and public-interest tech (sectoral models shown to raise long-term pay in MDRC randomized trials), paired with broader Federal Unemployment Tax Act training-approval standards and extended UI stipends so displaced workers keep income support during full-time programs. Finally, Congress should modernize ERISA multi-employer plan rules so unions and sectoral boards can run portable benefit pools across short-tenure jobs without weakening W-2 misclassification laws.

Recommendation 05 · Deepfakes, Scams & Creator Rights

5. Close civil deepfake gaps, stop voice-clone fraud, and protect creator likenesses

Building on the criminal penalties and 48-hour platform takedowns enacted in the 2025 TAKE IT DOWN Act, Congress should pass the DEFIANCE Act so intimate-deepfake victims can sue perpetrators directly in federal court, alongside a bipartisan NO FAKES Act paired with explicit parody and news safe harbors and a ban on coercive contract sign-aways so working creators and private citizens keep an enforceable consent right over AI clones of their voice and likeness.

Federal law should also stiffen wire-fraud penalties and telecom caller-authentication rules for AI voice-clone scams targeting seniors, require commercial AI generators to embed open synthetic-origin metadata (without personal-identity tracking), require large platforms to preserve it while barring intentional removal to defraud or impersonate, and support voluntary collective licensing markets for commercial creative archives without outlawing noncommercial open research.

Implementation Design

Trade-offs to weigh in policy design

01 · Permitting Scope

Fast-tracking clean energy infrastructure can fracture center-left coalitions if permitting bills sweep too broadly, as Time documented during the 2025 and 2026 permitting battles. Statutory fast-tracks must stay tightly scoped to clean transmission lines, reconductoring, dynamic line ratings, battery storage, and zero-carbon generation on low-conflict or disturbed sites so they never weaken NEPA review, Tribal consultation, or Clean Air Act enforcement on fossil projects, as demanded by the Sierra Club and frontline community groups.

02 · Tax Design & Training Scale

Market-oriented economists at ITIF and the American Enterprise Institute warn that literal "robot taxes" or domestic AI compute taxes are nearly impossible to define in tax law and risk pushing data centers abroad, while MDRC implementation studies caution that high-performing sectoral training programs are hard to scale rapidly without deep employer commitment. Pairing general bonus-depreciation reform with trust-fund-neutral payroll credits and rapid-reemployment wage insurance cushions displaced workers immediately without taxing domestic compute or stranding laid-off staff in low-return classes.

03 · Open Weights vs. Frontier Testing

Keeping model weights open remains essential to check Big Tech monopoly lock-in. To address national-security risks without choking off open science, federal pre-release testing mandates should apply only to frontier training runs above high statutory compute or biological-design thresholds capable of enabling catastrophic biological weapons or critical-infrastructure cyberattacks, with a clear statutory safe harbor for open-weight releases below those thresholds.

04 · Courtroom Accuracy & Audits

State court administrators and the National Center for State Courts warn that low-income litigants rarely have a second lawyer to catch AI hallucinations, which empirical tests by Stanford researchers still find in commercial legal tools. Publicly funded legal-aid software must carry mandatory independent accuracy audits, attorney-vetted template bounds for self-help tools, and human attorney sign-off on generative legal filings.

AI and computing

AI agents ("agentic" AI) and hallucinations
AI agents are software systems that go beyond answering chat prompts to carry out multi-step office tasks across external programs, such as processing insurance claims, reviewing files, or writing software. Hallucinations are plausible-sounding false facts, invented citations, or misstated holdings produced because generative AI predicts word patterns, a failure mode that persists even when tools retrieve verified legal records.
Open-weight models vs. closed APIs
An open-weight model releases its trained numerical parameters ("weights") publicly so universities, hospitals, and smaller firms can download and run the software on their own servers or local hardware. Closed APIs keep the model locked on a tech company's servers and charge customers ongoing fees for access.
Hyperscale data centers and NAIRR public compute
Hyperscale data centers are industrial server complexes built by major cloud providers that draw 20 to 1,000+ megawatts of electricity, comparable to a large town or major city. NAIRR (the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource) is an NSF- and DOE-led federal initiative coordinating national-lab supercomputers and donated cloud credits for academic, nonprofit, and startup researchers.
"Acqui-hires"
A deal structure (often called a reverse acqui-hire) where a tech giant hires away a startup's founders and core engineering team while licensing its technology to gain effective control without buying the company or triggering standard antitrust merger review.
Provenance (synthetic-origin) metadata
A tamper-evident technical record (such as the open C2PA standard) embedded in synthetic audio, video, or images that records which commercial AI tool generated or edited the file without needing to track the end user's personal identity.

Power grid, water, and air

PJM grid and wholesale capacity auctions
PJM Interconnection is America's largest regional grid operator, managing wholesale electricity across 13 Mid-Atlantic and Midwest states plus Washington, D.C. Its capacity auctions pay power plants in advance to guarantee enough supply during future peak demand; surging data-center power forecasts have driven those auction prices sharply higher on household bills.
FERC, state utility commissions, and 100% cost-causation tariffs
FERC (the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission) regulates interstate electricity transmission and wholesale power markets, while state public utility commissions set retail electricity rates. A 100% cost-causation tariff is a wholesale and retail rate rule requiring a giant new electricity user, such as a hyperscale data center, to pay the full cost of the grid upgrades and power capacity it triggers so residential bills do not rise.
Interconnection queues, Order 845, and generator replacement
The interconnection queue is the multi-year engineering waitlist new power projects and large loads face before plugging into the grid. FERC Order 845 ("surplus interconnection") lets new generation or batteries share unused hookup capacity at an operating power plant, while generator-replacement rules let new projects reuse the existing grid hookup when an older plant retires; both are fuel-neutral rules that solar and battery projects can use rapidly at operating or retiring fossil sites.
Peak load curtailment
A binding grid agreement where a data center trims non-urgent computing jobs or shifts to clean on-site battery storage (without running diesel backup generators) during the grid's highest-stress summer and winter hours so utilities do not have to build billions of dollars in extra fossil-fuel peaker plants.
Advanced reconductoring and dynamic line ratings
Advanced reconductoring replaces old steel-core wires on existing transmission towers with modern composite-core cables that carry roughly twice as much electricity without multi-year land fights for new towers. Dynamic line ratings use real-time wind and temperature sensors so grid operators can safely send more power through existing wires on cool or breezy days.
Enhanced geothermal
Clean baseload energy that uses modern directional rock-drilling techniques to tap natural heat miles underground and generate steady 24/7 carbon-free electricity with a small land footprint.
Closed-loop cooling, reclaimed water, and methane-turbine loopholes
Closed-loop cooling circulates liquid inside sealed pipes to cool servers instead of evaporating millions of gallons of municipal drinking water; reclaimed (recycled) water is treated municipal wastewater piped to industrial facilities for non-potable cooling. On air pollution, some fixed data centers have run trailer-mounted methane gas turbines without standard permits by claiming Clean Air Act nonroad-engine exemptions meant for movable machinery like bulldozers or short-term "temporary" stationary turbine rules.

Tax, labor, health, and law

100% bonus depreciation vs. economic depreciation ("so-so automation")
Bonus depreciation (alongside Section 174A software expensing) lets businesses deduct the full cost of new equipment and software in Year 1 instead of spreading deductions over the asset's useful working life (economic depreciation). Economists use "so-so automation" to describe mediocre software that barely matches human staff and gets deployed mainly because capital and software write-offs are taxed far more lightly than human wages.
"Bossware," wage insurance, and sectoral training
"Bossware" is workplace software that monitors keystrokes, cameras, or movement speed to enforce rigid quotas or automated discipline. Wage insurance is a temporary federal paycheck supplement (such as covering half of a pay drop up to an indexed annual cap for two years) when a laid-off worker starts a new job at lower pay. Sectoral training pairs public workforce funds directly with employers and unions to train and hire workers for specific job shortages.
WARN Act, FUTA, and ERISA multi-employer plans
The WARN Act currently requires large employers to give 60 days' notice before mass layoffs at a single physical worksite. FUTA (the Federal Unemployment Tax Act) sets federal baseline rules for state unemployment insurance. ERISA governs private workplace health and retirement benefits, though its portable multi-employer benefit pools are currently limited largely to union collective-bargaining agreements.
Byrne JAG, LSC, Daubert reliability, and Rule 707
Byrne JAG is the main Department of Justice grant program for state criminal-justice systems, while the Legal Services Corporation (LSC) funds civil legal aid for low-income households. Daubert is the federal courtroom standard requiring expert technical evidence to prove its reliability before reaching a jury; Proposed Rule 707 was a 2025-2026 draft federal evidence rule applying Daubert reliability checks to machine-generated trial evidence offered without a human expert witness.
Commercial data-broker loophole
The practice where intelligence agencies and police departments buy bulk phone-location, web-browsing, and personal records from commercial data brokers on the open market to track Americans without getting a Fourth Amendment court warrant.
Medicare Advantage prior authorization and WISeR
Prior authorization requires insurer sign-off before a doctor's prescribed treatment is covered. WISeR (Wasteful and Inappropriate Service Reduction) is a 2026 six-state federal pilot introducing private AI prior-authorization contractors into traditional Original Medicare.
